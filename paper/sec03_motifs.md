# 3. Results I: Motif Structure of the Fly Brain

## 3.1 Reciprocal pairs are strongly enriched
The directed triad census shows that the fly connectome is built from closed, mutually connected local structure. The reciprocity of the thresholded graph is r = 0.1398. The mutual-pair triad class (102) is enriched against all three nulls: z = +2,845 against the degree-preserving null and z = +1,355 against the compartment-preserving null. The enrichment appears in all 51 testable neuropils under the degree-preserving null (median z = 47.1). Survival under N3 means the effect cannot be explained solely by neurons sharing a compartment.

## 3.2 Open chains are suppressed
The complement of this pattern is just as consistent. The single-edge triad (012) is depleted (z_N3 = -1,355; median z = -46.5 across neuropils, depleted in all 51). The three open-chain classes are depleted as well (021D z_N3 = -280, 021U = -288, 021C = -282), in 47 to 49 of 51 neuropils. Triads in this brain are mostly either empty (003, z_N3 = +1,354) or closed. Open two-step chains are rare relative to a degree-matched expectation.

## 3.3 Feedforward loops concentrate in the visual pathway
The transitive feedforward loop (030T) is enriched against all three nulls (z_N3 = +132; z_N1 = +638). Its enrichment is not uniform. It concentrates in medulla and lamina neuropils and in the gnathal ganglia (ME_L +205, GNG +200, ME_R +190, LA_L +49) and is depleted in the antennal lobes and ellipsoid body. The cyclic triad (030C) behaves differently. It is enriched under N1 and N2 (z = +124) but reverses under N3 (z_N3 = -65). That enrichment was a product of compartment composition, and we report it as organization-dependent, not as a motif.

## 3.4 Modular organization
The graph has strong community structure. Across 25 seeded restarts, modularity ranged from 0.674 to 0.692 (mean 0.684), with 261 to 265 communities. The partition varies from restart to restart (mean pairwise variation of information 1.22), while its quality does not. We therefore claim modular organization of the graph, not any single partition.

## 3.5 The rich club is absent; hubs are under-connected
Contrary to the expectation that hubs form a densely interconnected core, normalized rich-club coefficients are about 1 at low degree (rho = 0.97, 1.00, 1.04, 0.98 at k = 10, 25, 50, 100) and then fall below the null (rho = 0.78, 0.58, 0.43, 0.31 at k = 200, 300, 400, 500). High-degree neurons are less connected to one another than their degrees alone predict. The sd across null replicates is below 1% of the mean at every k, so the ratios are stable despite only 5 nulls. We label this analysis exploratory: it was not among the confirmatory tests, and it differs in snapshot, threshold and null definition from the rich-club reported on the v630 snapshot, so we do not frame it as a contradiction.

## 3.6 Neurotransmitter-associated organization
Each connection record (one per neuron pair and neuropil, at least 5 synapses; 3,868,827 after skipping 1,051 without a prediction) was labeled with its dominant predicted neurotransmitter. Acetylcholine is most common (2,258,155 records, 58.4%), followed by GABA (865,318, 22.4%) and glutamate (654,183, 16.9%); octopamine, serotonin and dopamine together label 91,171 (2.4%). The composition varies strongly by region, and we give it in Section 3c.

## 3.7 What did not hold
Several expected effects were not supported. Enrichment profiles do not differ significantly across sensory, associative and motor neuropil classes (permutation p = 0.299), so no class-level architecture claim is made. Post-hoc single-motif class tests were also non-significant (030T p = 0.074). Under the within-neuropil N3 null, 9 neuropil-motif sign flips appeared for class 012, mostly in neuropils where N3 is near-degenerate; these are listed in the results file and treated as Tier 2. Eight small-count classes remain mixing-unresolved and carry no headline claim.

## 3.8 Robustness to the edge threshold
The headline signs do not depend on the 5-synapse cutoff. We repeated the triad census at stricter thresholds of 10 synapses (124,445 neurons, 1,066,822 edges) and 50 synapses (30,634 neurons, 66,438 edges), each against 10 degree-preserving nulls. The 5-synapse graph is a superset of both, so these graphs are nested subsets of it. A fourth graph at the loosest threshold, 1 synapse, was built directly from the raw connection table (all 15,091,983 connected neuron pairs, 139,255 neurons); it is not a subset of the others by construction but contains them.

At 10 synapses the confirmatory classes keep their direction: empty triads z = +1,707, single edges -1,719, mutual pairs +1,709, open chains -340 (021D), -226 (021U) and -304 (021C), and the feedforward loop +196. At 50 synapses the picture is the same, with smaller magnitudes as the graph shrinks: empty +351, single edge -352, mutual pair +351, open chains -171, -148 and -152, feedforward loop +677. The feedforward loop is enriched at every threshold, though its z-score is not monotonic (+638 at 5 synapses, +196 at 10, +677 at 50, all under the degree-preserving null). The cyclic triad stays positive under this null at every threshold (+37 at 10, +73 at 50), as it did at 5 (+124 under the degree null); its reversal appears only under the compartment-preserving null (Section 3.3). These nulls use only 10 replicates, so the z-scores are descriptive. The signs, not the magnitudes, are the finding. The class 300 z-score at 50 synapses is undefined because its null variance is zero.

## 3.9 Four-node structure follows the triad pattern
To test whether the triad findings extend to larger patterns, we counted all directed four-node subgraphs in 20 neuropils of 189 to 2,427 neurons and 307 to 15,274 edges each (EB, NO, VES_L, VES_R, SIP_R, MB_VL_R, WED_R, OCG, MB_CA_R, MB_CA_L, AMMC_R, CRE_L, CRE_R, ICL_L, ICL_R, LA_R, LA_L, IPS_R, AOTU_L, SPS_L), and compared them with 10 degree-preserving nulls per neuropil. Only small neuropils were feasible: one census of the 15,274-edge ellipsoid body took 11 seconds, and cost grows steeply with edge count, so the whole-brain graph and the 31 larger neuropils were out of reach. This is an exploratory analysis with few nulls, and it makes no confirmatory claim.

Of the 199 connected four-node classes, 135 were observable in at least 15 of the 20 neuropils. Individual classes are heterogeneous across neuropils. Only one class was enriched (z above 3) in at least 15 neuropils without ever being depleted (five edges, two mutual pairs), and three sparse three-edge classes with no mutual pair were depleted in the same sense. In an earlier pass over the first eight neuropils, six enriched and eight depleted classes met the same rule; adding twelve neuropils removed most of them, so we make no claim about individual four-node classes.

The structural trend is stable. Across classes, the median z-score rises with the number of mutual pairs: -3.7 for classes with none, +1.0 with one, +5.5 with two, +9.9 with three. It rises with the number of edges too, from -9.8 at three edges to +7.7 at eight. Larger patterns are therefore built from the same ingredients as the triads, with mutual pairs enriched and sparse open patterns suppressed. Individual classes vary by neuropil, so this is a statement about the gradient and not about particular four-node motifs. It supports using reciprocal coupling and bounded chain depth as the two organizing rules in Section 4.

## 3.10 Sensitivity to the number of null graphs
The primary analysis uses 100 null graphs per family. To check that z-scores do not depend on that choice, we drew 100 further, independent density-matched random graphs (new seeds) and recomputed the z-scores for the eight headline-safe classes against them alone. Magnitudes moved by 1% to 20% (empty triads and mutual pairs 12.7%, single edges 12.7%, open chains 1.2%, 6.5% and 10.7%, feedforward loop 0.7%, cyclic triad 19.7%), as expected when the null standard deviation is estimated from 100 samples. Every sign matched, and every z-score stayed in the thousands, far beyond any significance threshold: mutual pairs +12,362 against +14,157, feedforward loop +42,449 against +42,163. For the degree-preserving family we drew 40 further nulls (about 29 seconds each) with the C engine and a new set of seeds, and recomputed the same z-scores against them alone. Signs again all match. Magnitudes differ more than for the density-matched family: 11% to 42% (mutual pairs 2,845 against 4,025, single edges -2,877 against -4,078, feedforward loop 638 against 822, open chains -390, -407 and -389 against -434, -471 and -444, cyclic triad 124 against 109). Two things contribute and we cannot separate them with these data. The extra batch has only 40 nulls, and it uses a different engine from the locked 100, whose batched implementation checks duplicates against a stale edge set and whose random stream differs. Both engines implement the same null definition, but their finite-sample distributions need not agree exactly. Every z-score remains in the hundreds to thousands and keeps its sign, so the confirmatory calls do not change. Magnitudes of z-scores are descriptive; the conclusions rest on signs and on the survival of each class across nulls.

At 1 synapse the signs are again unchanged, against only 3 degree-preserving nulls (each needing about 14 minutes of compute at this size, so the z-scores are rough). Mutual pairs are enriched (z = +19,968), empty triads are enriched (+19,914), single edges are depleted (-19,811), the open chains are depleted (021D -10,600, 021U -11,489, 021C -12,719), and the feedforward loop is enriched (+1,967). The cyclic triad is enriched (+1,118) as it was under the degree null at the other thresholds. Across thresholds of 1, 5, 10 and 50 synapses the direction of every confirmatory class is therefore the same. The magnitudes grow with the size of the graph, which is expected of a z-score, and they should not be compared across thresholds.

## 3.11 Weights: reciprocal connections are coordinated in strength
All results so far treat an edge as present or absent. The connectome also records how many synapses each connection has, so we asked whether the weights carry the same reciprocal structure. We kept the graph fixed and permuted the weights across edges 200 times (seeded), which preserves every degree, every motif count and the weight distribution while destroying any relationship between weight and position.

The weighted reciprocity measures the share of total synapse weight that sits in reciprocated connections,

(F11)  r_w = 2 * sum over mutual pairs of min(w_ij, w_ji) / sum over all edges of w_ij.

Its observed value is 0.1157, against 0.0813 in the permuted graphs (permutation sd 0.0001, z = +369). It is lower than the count-based reciprocity of 0.1398 because the weaker side of a pair counts, which is expected. More interesting is the coordination within pairs. Across the 188,724 mutual pairs, the Spearman correlation between the weight of i to j and the weight of j to i is +0.189 (permutation mean +0.0003, sd 0.0022, z = +85). The two directions of a reciprocal pair therefore tend to have similar strength, far beyond what the weight distribution alone predicts. Edges that belong to a mutual pair also have a higher median weight than other edges (10 synapses against 8; permuted graphs give 8 for both).

These weighted results are descriptive. The permutation fixes topology, so they speak only to the relationship between weight and reciprocity and not to whether weighted motifs are enriched in a null graph with matched weight structure. The frozen mapping of Section 4 gave reciprocal units independent weights and rejected weight-tied pairs as a stronger claim than the data then supported. A coordination of +0.19 is modest, so it does not by itself justify tying weights, but it identifies weight-coupled reciprocal pairs as an untested alternative mapping.
