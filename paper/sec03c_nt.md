# 3c. Predicted-Neurotransmitter Organization

Each connection in the release carries six averaged neurotransmitter predictions from a model trained on synaptic images (acetylcholine, GABA, glutamate, octopamine, serotonin, dopamine). We labeled each edge with the neurotransmitter of highest predicted average. These are model predictions, not measurements of the neurotransmitter or its effect on the postsynaptic cell. In particular, we do not infer excitation from acetylcholine or inhibition from GABA, since the sign of a connection depends on the receptors of the target.

## 3c.1 Whole-brain composition
The labels are counted on the connection records of the release (one record per neuron pair and neuropil, with at least 5 synapses), which number 3,868,827 after skipping 1,051 records with no prediction. This is a different unit from the 2,700,513 neuron-to-neuron edges of the motif analysis, which sum synapses across neuropils, so the counts below are not fractions of that edge set. Acetylcholine is the dominant label on 2,258,155 records (58.4%), GABA on 865,318 (22.4%), and glutamate on 654,183 (16.9%). Octopamine, serotonin and dopamine together label 91,171 records (2.4%). Weighting each record by its synapse count gives the same ordering: 19.5 million synapses are acetylcholine-dominant, 8.3 million GABA-dominant, 5.6 million glutamate-dominant, and 0.64 million belong to the three modulators combined (octopamine 95,762, serotonin 340,040, dopamine 208,626).

## 3c.2 The composition varies strongly by region
Across the 79 regions in the table (including records not assigned to a single neuropil), the median region has 19.6% GABA-dominant records, 57.6% acetylcholine-dominant, and 16.5% glutamate-dominant. The spread is wide. The ellipsoid body is the clear outlier on the GABA side, with 74.9% GABA-dominant edges out of 17,022 and only 20.4% acetylcholine-dominant. It is followed by the left and right antennal mechanosensory and motor centers (AMMC_L 48.0%, AMMC_R 38.4%), the saddle (SAD 33.9%), and the left bulb (BU_L 33.7%). At the other end, the protocerebral bridge (PB 4.9%) and the fan-shaped body (FB 6.2%) have the fewest GABA-dominant edges, and they have the most glutamate-dominant edges (FB 44.0%, PB 38.7%). The mushroom body calyces and lobes lean strongly acetylcholine-dominant (MB_CA_L 78.6%, MB_CA_R 78.0%, MB_VL_R 76.3%), as does the left lamina (73.3%).

The modulatory classes concentrate in specific regions. The three modulators together make up 29.9% of labeled records in the optic glomeruli (OCG), 25.1% of records not assigned to a neuropil, and 19.2% and 17.6% in the left and right antennal lobes, against a median near zero elsewhere.

## 3c.3 Reading this against the motif results
The neurotransmitter labels give a second view of the same regional heterogeneity that the feedforward-loop split showed in Section 3b.2. The ellipsoid body, where the feedforward loop is most depleted (z = -50.5), is also the region with by far the highest share of GABA-dominant edges. The antennal lobes, also depleted for the loop (z near -24), have a high modulatory share (second only to OCG and the unassigned records). Two regions are too few to support a rule. We note the coincidence and make no claim that neurotransmitter content causes the motif profile. The data do not test it, and the number of regions is small. Predicted-neurotransmitter structure also raised, in the preregistration, the question of building a signed network (Section 7.3). The structure is clear enough to justify one, and we did not build it.
