# 7. Discussion

## 7.1 What the structure says about the fly brain
The central structural finding is simple and survives every check we ran: the fly connectome is built from mutual pairs and closed local structure, and it avoids open chains. The pattern appears against three different null models, in all 51 testable neuropils for the mutual pair and the single-edge triad, at thresholds of 5, 10 and 50 synapses, and in the four-node census, where enrichment rises steadily with the number of mutual pairs. The feedforward loop adds a second, narrower finding: it is enriched overall but concentrates in the visual pathway, which is consistent with a role in processing structured sensory input. The absence of a rich club and the depletion of connections among the highest-degree neurons show that hubs in this brain do not form a densely interconnected core.

The comparison with the cyclic triad is instructive in its own right. A pattern that looks like a motif under a degree-preserving null can disappear when compartments are held fixed, so enrichment claims in connectomes need a compartment-aware null to mean anything.

## 7.2 What the networks say about computation
The functional results are narrower than the structural ones. A connectome-derived sparse mask degrades more gradually under unit ablation than a random mask of the same size, and this holds on two tasks. But the same mask is less accurate than a random mask at every noise level, learns more slowly, and offers no accuracy gain when imposed on a dense-pretrained network. Dense and convolutional networks are both more accurate and relatively more robust. We read the retention advantage as a consequence of the fly mask's low accuracy and limited edge budget, which leaves less to lose, and not as evidence that connectome wiring confers robustness. The hypothesis that the specific biological structures improve noise robustness, learning speed or efficiency was tested directly and not supported.

## 7.3 Limitations
The mapping from motifs to masks is one of several possible, and we froze a single, defensible rule. A different mapping, for example one that preserves directed edge weights or signs, could behave differently. Signed (excitatory and inhibitory) wiring was admitted in the preregistration but never trained, because it would have duplicated an unsigned arm without a predicted-neurotransmitter sign export. Task scale is small (MNIST-class and a five-class CIFAR subset), at one hidden-layer geometry. The threshold sweep covers 10 and 50 synapses and not 1. The four-node census covers small neuropils only. Cell-type stratification awaits an annotation source we could not access. Rich-club and four-node analyses are exploratory and use few null replicates.

## 7.4 Conclusion
The fly brain's wiring has clear, robust statistical structure: reciprocal, closed and modular, with no rich club. Using that structure to build artificial networks gave a modest and narrow robustness effect within sparse networks, and no accuracy or efficiency advantage over simpler alternatives. Both findings are reported at full strength, and the second is what keeps the first honest.
